Crypto Grid Trading Calculator

Plan a grid trading bot before you run it. Enter your price range, number of grids, investment and exchange fee, and see the profit per grid after fees, profit per completed buy-sell cycle, an estimate of daily and monthly income from how often the price crosses a grid, and what your position is worth if the price drops to the bottom of the range. It handles arithmetic (equal dollar gaps) and geometric (equal percentage gaps) grids. Everything runs in your browser.





How grid trading profit is calculated

A grid bot splits your range into levels and places a buy order at each level, with a sell one grid higher. Each time the price drops to a level and climbs back one step, the bot completes a cycle and keeps the gap. In an arithmetic grid every gap is the same dollar amount, (upper − lower) ÷ grids, so the percentage profit is biggest near the bottom of the range and smallest near the top. In a geometric grid each level is the previous one times (upper ÷ lower)^(1 ÷ grids), so every grid earns the same percentage. Fees are paid on both the buy and the sell, so the profit per grid is gap % − 2 × fee %, and the dollar profit per cycle is that times the money placed on each grid (investment ÷ grids).

Worked example

A BTC grid from $55,000 to $75,000 with 40 arithmetic grids has a $500 gap. That is about 0.67% per grid at the top and 0.91% at the bottom, or roughly 0.47% to 0.71% after a 0.1% fee on each side. With $2,000 spread over 40 grids ($50 each), each completed cycle earns about $0.24 to $0.35. At 3 cycles a day that is around $0.89 a day, or about $27 a month, before any change in the coin's price.

The risk the profit number hides

  • If the price falls below your lower bound, the bot has bought at every level and holds coins worth less than you paid. The calculator shows that bottom-of-range value so you can see the drawdown.
  • If the price breaks above your upper bound, the bot has sold everything and stops earning while the coin keeps rising.
  • More grids means smaller gaps and more cycles, but fees eat a bigger share of each one. Keep the profit per grid well above twice the fee.
  • Cycles per day is a guess. Sideways, choppy markets give many cycles; trending markets give few.

FAQ

How much can a crypto grid bot make?

Profit per completed cycle is the grid gap percentage minus fees on both sides, times the money on each grid. Total profit depends on how often the price crosses grid levels, which is high in sideways markets and low in strong trends. Price moves outside your range can outweigh grid profit.

Is an arithmetic or geometric grid better?

An arithmetic grid uses equal dollar gaps, so it earns more percentage per grid near the bottom of the range. A geometric grid uses equal percentage gaps, so every grid earns the same percentage. Geometric grids are often preferred for wide ranges.

How many grids should I use?

Enough that the profit per grid stays comfortably above twice your trading fee. Too many grids makes each gap tiny so fees eat most of the profit; too few grids means fewer cycles.
