How portfolio rebalancing works

Add up the value of everything to get the portfolio total (plus any new cash). Each asset's target value = total × target %. The trade for each asset is target value − current value: a positive number is a buy, a negative one is a sell. Drift is the current weight minus the target weight, in percentage points. A common rule is to rebalance only when an asset drifts past a band, such as 5 points, rather than on every small move, which keeps trades and fees down.

In buy-only mode no coin is sold. New cash goes to the underweight assets in proportion to how far each one is below its target. If the cash is more than enough to close every gap, the rest is split by your target percentages.

Worked example

A $10,000 portfolio with a 50/30/10/10 target holds $6,500 BTC, $2,000 ETH, $1,500 SOL and no USDC. Bitcoin has run up to 65% (+15 points) and ETH has fallen to 20%. A full rebalance means selling $1,500 BTC and $500 SOL, and buying $1,000 ETH and $1,000 USDC: 4 trades, $4,000 traded and about $4 in fees at 0.1%. Adding $5,000 of new cash instead fixes it with no sales: buy $1,000 BTC, $2,500 ETH and $1,500 USDC, and the $15,000 portfolio lands exactly on 50/30/10/10.

FAQ

How do you calculate a crypto portfolio rebalance?

Add up the total value, multiply it by each asset's target percentage to get the target value, then subtract the current value. Positive means buy that much, negative means sell. Example: a $10,000 portfolio with a 50% Bitcoin target should hold $5,000 of BTC; if you hold $6,500, sell $1,500.

How often should I rebalance my crypto portfolio?

There is no single right answer. Some people rebalance on a calendar (monthly or quarterly), others only when an asset drifts past a band such as 5 percentage points. Bands usually mean fewer trades and lower fees in a volatile market like crypto.

Can I rebalance without selling?

Yes. Put new money into the underweight assets instead of selling the overweight ones. Use buy-only mode above: it shows how to split the cash and how close to target you end up. It takes more cash when one coin has run up a lot.

Is rebalancing crypto a taxable event?

In the US, selling or swapping one crypto for another is a taxable disposal, so a full rebalance can create capital gains (or losses). Buying with new cash is not a sale. Rules differ by country; check with a tax professional.

Should stablecoins be part of my target allocation?

Many people keep a stablecoin or cash slice so they have dry powder to buy dips and a buffer that does not swing with the market. Whether and how much is a personal choice; this calculator works with any mix you enter.
