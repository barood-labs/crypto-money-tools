Why this list is complete

Who first funded each recipient wallet with BNB

The task asks which wallet first sent each recipient BNB, and whether any recipients share one. For each recipient the BNB balance was binary-searched back through history to the first block in which it became non-zero; the funding transaction was then read in full and its value matched against that balance change. Where a wallet never held BNB, that is reported as a negative rather than guessed at, because a token transfer needs no gas from the receiver.

The grouping that the single day hides

Grouping only this day’s recipients understates the link, because the same funder can pay different wallets on different days. Looking at all three audited days together, these funders paid more than one recipient:

  • the audited treasury itself funded 3 recipient wallets with gas: 0x8186bdc89e5893393136265f88ba3719bf453527, 0xca58696564492ef9c3229c89072365b253bda246, 0xfb200657f7266157a45dfcc6c09690726daa24e6

The treasury paying a wallet’s gas is the clearest link in this whole audit: the operator of the treasury chose to fund those wallets so they could transact, which is not something that happens to an unrelated third party.

What this audit cannot conclude

  • It covers one COT day. Transfers on other days are outside the window and are not counted here.
  • It describes FLAPJAX movements only. BNB or other tokens moving in or out of the same addresses are not part of this audit.
  • A shared first BNB funder is evidence that two wallets were set up by the same hand. The absence of a funder is not evidence that wallets are unrelated, because a wallet can receive tokens without ever holding BNB.
  • Nothing here identifies a person. Addresses are reported as addresses.

How to reproduce every number

  1. Take the first and last block above and confirm their timestamps on BscScan.
  2. Call eth_getLogs on token 0x90c8889f428F9Ebb77BB8f15CAD3a50a9aC680df over that range with topic0 = 0xddf252ad1be2c89b69c2b068fc378daa952ba7f163c4a11628f55a4df523b3ef (the ERC-20 Transfer signature).
  3. Keep the events whose topics[1] is the zero-padded treasury address 7c34e9e21ee28a49ff0b84b61774119e6633359f. That is the set below: 18 events. Events with the treasury in topics[2] are money coming in and are counted separately in the reconciliation above.
  4. Read each amount from the data word, divide by 1018 (decimals confirmed by calling 0x313ce567).
  5. For the check, call 0x70a08231 (balanceOf) with the treasury address at block 130413457 and at block 130605332, and subtract.

One practical warning for anyone repeating this: several free public BSC nodes return a truncated eth_getLogs result with no error at all, silently dropping rows on wide block ranges. That is why the balance-delta check above exists, and why the scan behind this page was cross-checked against it before publishing.
