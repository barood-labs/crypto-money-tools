Prop Firm Consistency Rule Calculator

Many prop firms have a consistency rule: your single best day can't be more than a set share of your total profit (often somewhere between 30% and 50%) before you can pass an evaluation or request a payout. Enter your best day, your total profit and your firm's limit to see whether you pass, how much more profit you need, and the biggest day you can take today without making it worse. It runs in your browser.



How the consistency rule is calculated

Consistency % = best day profit / total profit. If your best day was $1,200 and your total profit is $2,500, your best day is 48% of the total. With a 40% limit you fail for now, and you need total profit of at least best day / limit = $1,200 / 0.40 = $3,000, which is $500 more, made on days smaller than $1,200.

The trap: one big day can reset the clock

If today's profit becomes your new best day, the target moves up. To keep today's result from making things worse, keep it at or below limit x total / (1 - limit). With $2,500 total and a 40% limit that is about $1,667. Many traders hit a lock-out after one huge day, then have to grind out several smaller days to dilute it. Steady sizing beats a lucky day here.

Tips for passing consistency rules

  • Read your firm's exact rule: some apply it to the evaluation, some only to funded payouts, and some count the best day against the profit target instead of total profit.
  • Cap your daily profit target (for example, stop at 20-30% of the payout goal) and use a position size calculator so one trade can't dominate.
  • Know your drawdown room first with the prop firm drawdown calculator, and check whether the account is worth it with the challenge cost calculator.

FAQ

What is a prop firm consistency rule?

It limits how much of your total profit can come from a single day. For example, with a 40% rule, a $1,000 best day needs at least $2,500 in total profit.

How do I fix a failed consistency rule?

Keep trading and add profit on days smaller than your best day until best day divided by total profit is at or under the limit. Avoid a new, bigger best day, which raises the target.

Does a losing day affect the consistency rule?

Yes. Losses lower your total profit, which raises your best day's share of it, so you will need more profit to get back under the limit.
