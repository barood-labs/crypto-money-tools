How to Make a Monthly Budget That Actually Works

A budget is just a plan for where your money goes before the month starts. The method below takes about 30 minutes the first time and 10 minutes a month after that. Use the free calculator to get your starting numbers.

Step 1: Use take-home pay, not salary

Budget the money that actually lands in your account after tax, retirement contributions and insurance. If your income varies (freelance, tips, commission), use your lowest month from the last six, and treat anything above it as a bonus to assign when it arrives.

Step 2: List fixed bills first

Rent or mortgage, utilities, phone, insurance, car payment, minimum debt payments, childcare and subscriptions. These are your "needs" and are the hardest to change quickly. Pull them from your last two bank statements so nothing is missed.

Step 3: Estimate variable spending from real data

Groceries, fuel, eating out and shopping vary month to month. Don't guess: add up the last 60–90 days and divide by the number of months. Most people underestimate these by 20–40% the first time.

Step 4: Add the expenses people forget

  • Annual bills (car registration, Amazon Prime, software, insurance paid yearly): divide by 12.
  • Gifts and holidays: birthdays and December add up fast.
  • Car maintenance and tires, medical co-pays, pet costs.
  • Irregular kid costs: school trips, sports fees, clothes.

These are best handled with sinking funds, small monthly set-asides for known future costs.

Step 5: Pick a framework

MethodHow it worksBest for
50/30/2050% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings and extra debt paymentsBeginners, steady income
Zero-basedEvery dollar gets a job until income minus plan = $0Tight budgets, paying off debt
Pay yourself firstAutomate savings on payday, spend the rest freelyPeople who hate tracking
Envelope / cash stuffingFixed cash per category; when it's gone, it's goneOverspending on groceries or fun money

If your needs are above 50% (common in high-rent cities), that's fine. Try 60/20/20 or 70/20/10 and work on lowering the big three: housing, transport and food.

Step 6: Track and review weekly

A budget fails when you stop looking at it. Spend five minutes each Sunday logging spending and checking which categories are running hot. At month end, move leftover money to savings or debt, and adjust next month's numbers.

FAQ

What is the 50/30/20 rule?

It splits take-home pay into 50% for needs (housing, bills, groceries), 30% for wants (eating out, hobbies) and 20% for savings and extra debt payments. It is a starting point, not a strict rule.

How much should I budget for groceries?

Use your own average from the last two or three months of bank statements. Then set a slightly lower target and track it weekly.

What if my income changes every month?

Budget from your lowest recent month, cover needs first, and assign extra income to savings or debt when it arrives.
