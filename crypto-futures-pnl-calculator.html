How futures profit and ROE are calculated

Your coin quantity is position size / entry price. Gross profit is quantity x (exit - entry) for a long and quantity x (entry - exit) for a short. Fees are charged on the notional value at both entry and exit. ROE (return on equity) is net profit divided by the margin you put up, which is position size / leverage. That is why a 3% move at 10x shows as roughly a 30% ROE, and why the same move against you costs 30% of your margin.

Leverage does not change your profit, only your margin

A $10,000 position makes the same dollar profit at 2x or 20x. Higher leverage just means less margin is locked up, so the ROE looks bigger and liquidation sits much closer to your entry. FAQ

How do you calculate profit on a crypto futures trade?

Quantity = position size / entry price. Long profit = quantity x (exit - entry); short profit = quantity x (entry - exit). Subtract the trading fee at entry and at exit to get net profit.

What is ROE in crypto futures?

ROE is return on equity: net profit divided by the initial margin (position size / leverage). At 10x, a 2% price move is roughly a 20% ROE before fees.

Does higher leverage increase profit?

Not for the same position size. Leverage only lowers the margin you post, which raises ROE and moves your liquidation price closer to entry.
