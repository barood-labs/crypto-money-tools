Sinking Funds Calculator (and What to Save For)

A sinking fund is money you save a little at a time for a cost you know is coming, like Christmas, car insurance or a vacation. It's the main reason budgets survive "surprise" expenses that weren't really surprises.

Common sinking fund categories

  • Car: insurance, registration, repairs, tires, next car down payment
  • Home: repairs, appliances, property tax, HOA, furniture
  • Holidays & gifts: Christmas, birthdays, weddings you're attending
  • Health: deductibles, dental, glasses, vet bills
  • Annual subscriptions: software, memberships, Prime, domain names
  • Kids: school supplies, sports, camps, clothing
  • Fun: vacation, concerts, a new phone or laptop

How to manage them

You don't need a separate bank account for each one. Many people use one savings account and track the balances in a spreadsheet, or use a bank that allows multiple "buckets". Cash stuffers use labeled envelopes. Whatever you use, write the target and date next to each fund so you can see whether you're on track.

Sinking fund vs emergency fund

A sinking fund is for planned costs. An emergency fund is for unplanned ones like job loss. Keep them separate so a planned vacation doesn't drain the money meant for a real emergency.

FAQ

What is a sinking fund?

Money saved monthly for a known future expense, so the cost is already covered when it arrives.

How many sinking funds should I have?

Start with the three to five largest irregular costs you had last year, then add more as needed.
