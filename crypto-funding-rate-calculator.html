Crypto Funding Rate Calculator

Work out how much funding you pay or receive on a perpetual futures position. Enter your position size, the funding rate per period and how long you plan to hold. Funding is charged on the full position value, not your margin, so even small rates add up on leveraged trades. Everything runs in your browser.



How perpetual funding works

Perpetual futures have no expiry, so exchanges use a funding payment to keep the contract price near the spot price. When the rate is positive, longs pay shorts. When it is negative, shorts pay longs. The payment each period is position value x funding rate, and it usually happens every 8 hours, three times a day. A 0.01% rate on a $10,000 position is $1 per period, about $3 a day and roughly $110 a year, or about 11% of a $1,000 margin.

Why it matters for leveraged trades

Because funding is charged on the notional value, its cost against your margin scales with leverage. At 10x, a 0.01% rate every 8 hours eats around 11% of your margin per year, and during hot markets rates of 0.05% to 0.1% per period are common, which can cost several percent of your margin in a week. Funding and taxes

Funding you receive is generally income and funding you pay is a trading cost. Keep a record of every payment so your profit and loss is right at tax time.

FAQ

How is the crypto funding fee calculated?

Funding fee = position value x funding rate. A $10,000 position at 0.01% pays or receives $1 each funding period. Most exchanges settle funding every 8 hours.

Do longs or shorts pay funding?

When the funding rate is positive, longs pay shorts. When it is negative, shorts pay longs. You only pay or receive it if you hold the position at the funding time.

What is the annual cost of a 0.01% funding rate?

With funding every 8 hours, 0.01% per period is about 0.03% a day and about 10.95% a year of the position value.
