Debt Snowball vs Avalanche: Free Side-by-Side Calculator

Both methods pay the minimum on every debt and put every extra dollar on one target debt. When that debt is gone, its payment rolls into the next one. The only difference is which debt you target first.

  • Snowball: smallest balance first. You get quick wins, which keeps many people motivated.
  • Avalanche: highest interest rate first. Mathematically it always costs the same or less interest.

Which one should you choose?

Run your numbers above. If the avalanche saves only a small amount (often under a few hundred dollars when rates are similar), the snowball's early wins may be worth it. If you have a high-rate credit card with a big balance, the avalanche usually saves much more. The method you'll stick with beats the method that's optimal on paper.

How to speed up either method

  • Find extra money: one cancelled subscription or a cheaper phone plan can add $30–$80 a month.
  • Put windfalls (tax refunds, bonuses) straight onto the target debt.
  • Ask your card issuer for a lower APR. It costs nothing to ask.
  • Keep a small starter emergency fund so a surprise bill doesn't go back on the card. See the emergency fund calculator.
  • Stop adding new debt while you pay down old debt.

Common mistakes

  • Lowering your total payment when a debt is paid off. Keep the total the same so the payment "snowballs".
  • Forgetting that minimums on some cards drop as the balance drops. Keep paying the original amount.
  • Ignoring promotional 0% rates that are about to expire. Treat the post-promo rate as the real rate.

FAQ

Is the debt snowball or avalanche better?

Avalanche costs less interest. Snowball gives faster early wins. If the interest difference is small, choose the one you'll stick with.

What happens when a debt is paid off?

Its minimum payment is added to the payment on the next target debt, so your total monthly payment stays the same.
