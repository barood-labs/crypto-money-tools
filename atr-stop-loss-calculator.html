ATR Stop Loss Calculator

Set a stop loss based on how much a market actually moves. Enter your entry price, the Average True Range (ATR) and a multiplier to get the stop price, the stop distance and the position size that keeps your loss to a fixed share of your account. Don't know the ATR? Paste recent high, low and close candles and the calculator works it out. Everything runs in your browser.




How the ATR stop loss is calculated

The true range of a candle is the largest of high − low, |high − previous close| and |low − previous close|. The ATR is the average true range over the last N candles (14 by default, smoothed the Wilder way). A long stop sits at entry − ATR × multiplier; a short stop at entry + ATR × multiplier. Position size is account × risk % ÷ stop distance, so a volatile market automatically gets a smaller position.

Worked example

You want to buy BTC at $70,000. The daily ATR is $1,500 and you use a 2× multiplier, so the stop goes $3,000 below entry at $67,000 (4.29% away). Risking 1% of a $10,000 account is $100, so the position is 0.0333 BTC (about $2,333). A 2R target is $76,000.

Choosing a multiplier

  • 1-1.5× ATR: tight stops for intraday scalps; you get stopped out more often.
  • 2× ATR: the common default for swing trades; gives normal noise room to breathe.
  • 3× ATR: wide trailing stops for trend following (the "chandelier exit" idea).

FAQ

How do you calculate an ATR stop loss?

Multiply the Average True Range by your chosen multiplier (often 2) and subtract it from the entry price for a long, or add it for a short. With a $1,500 ATR and 2x, a long from $70,000 gets a stop at $67,000.

What is a good ATR multiplier for a stop loss?

Many swing traders use 2x ATR. Intraday traders often use 1 to 1.5x, and trend followers use 3x or more as a trailing stop. Wider stops mean smaller position sizes for the same dollar risk.

Which timeframe ATR should I use?

Use the ATR from the chart timeframe you trade on: daily ATR for swing trades, 1-hour or 15-minute ATR for day trades. Crypto daily candles usually close at 00:00 UTC.

How does ATR help with position sizing?

Divide the amount you are willing to lose by the ATR-based stop distance. When volatility rises the stop gets wider and the position shrinks, so your dollar risk stays the same.
