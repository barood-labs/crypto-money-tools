How the risk to reward ratio is calculated

Risk is the distance from your entry to your stop loss, and reward is the distance from your entry to your take profit. The ratio is reward / risk, usually written as 1:R. A trade risking $100 to make $300 is 1:3. The win rate you need to break even is 1 / (1 + R), so at 1:3 you only need to be right 25% of the time, while at 1:1 you need more than 50%. Trading fees shrink the reward and grow the risk, so this calculator shows the ratio both before and after fees.

Expectancy: is the setup profitable over many trades?

If you enter your historical win rate, the calculator also shows your expectancy: win rate x net reward minus loss rate x net risk. A positive number means the setup makes money on average over many trades. FAQ

How do you calculate the risk to reward ratio?

Risk = |entry - stop loss| and reward = |take profit - entry|. The ratio is reward divided by risk. Entry $100, stop $95 and target $115 gives 5 risk and 15 reward, a 1:3 risk to reward ratio.

What win rate do I need for a 1:2 risk to reward?

Break-even win rate = 1 / (1 + R). At 1:2 that is 1/3, about 33.3%, before fees. Any win rate above that is profitable on average.

What is a good risk to reward ratio?

Many traders look for at least 1:2, but the right ratio depends on your win rate. A 1:1.5 setup that wins 55% of the time beats a 1:3 setup that wins 20%. Check expectancy, not the ratio alone.
