How the gain needed to recover is calculated

Drawdown % = (peak − current) ÷ peak. Gain needed % = peak ÷ current − 1. The two are not the same number because the recovery starts from a smaller balance: a 50% loss needs a 100% gain, a 75% loss needs 300% and a 90% loss needs 900%. At a steady return of r per period, periods to recover = ln(peak ÷ current) ÷ ln(1 + r). If each winning trade risks k% of the account and makes R times that, each win adds k × R %, so the number of wins in a row needed is ln(peak ÷ current) ÷ ln(1 + k × R), rounded up. A streak of n losses at k% risk leaves you down 1 − (1 − k)n.

Worked example

An account peaked at $10,000 and is now $7,000: a 30% drawdown. Getting back needs a 42.86% gain ($3,000 on $7,000). At 3% a month that takes about 12.1 months. Risking 1% per trade for 2R, each win adds 2%, so it takes 19 winning trades in a row (more once losses are mixed in). At the same 1% risk, 10 losses in a row cost only 9.6%, which is why small risk per trade keeps drawdowns recoverable. Halve the balance instead ($10,000 to $5,000) and you need a 100% gain.

FAQ

How much do I need to gain to recover from a loss?

Divide your peak by your current balance and subtract 1. A 10% loss needs an 11.1% gain, 20% needs 25%, 30% needs 42.9%, 50% needs 100% and 90% needs 900%. The deeper the drawdown, the faster the required gain grows.

Why does a 50% loss need a 100% gain?

Because the gain is earned on the smaller balance. $10,000 that falls 50% is $5,000, and $5,000 has to double (a 100% gain) to get back to $10,000.

How long does it take to recover from a drawdown?

Divide the natural log of peak over current balance by the natural log of 1 plus your return per period. A 30% drawdown at a steady 3% a month takes about 12.1 months. Real returns are uneven, so treat this as a rough guide.

What is a maximum drawdown?

The largest fall from a peak to a low before a new peak is made, shown as a percentage of the peak. Traders and fund managers use it to judge risk, and prop firms set a maximum drawdown limit that ends the account if it is breached.

How do I avoid big drawdowns?

Keep risk per trade small (1% or less is common), use a stop loss on every trade, stop trading for the day or week after a set loss, and review your journal to find the setups that lose most. At 1% risk, 10 losses in a row cost about 9.6%; at 5% risk they cost about 40%.
