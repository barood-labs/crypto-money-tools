How trading expectancy is calculated

Expectancy = (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss) − fees. It is the average amount you make or lose per trade over many trades. Dividing it by your average loss gives expectancy in R (multiples of what you risk). Profit factor is gross wins ÷ gross losses; above 1 the system makes money. The break-even win rate is average loss ÷ (average win + average loss), the lowest win rate at which you stop losing.

Worked example

You win 45% of trades, the average win is $300 and the average loss is $150, with $5 of fees per trade. Expectancy is 0.45 × 300 − 0.55 × 150 − 5 = $47.50 per trade (0.32R). Profit factor is 135 ÷ 82.5 = 1.64, and you would break even at a win rate of just 33.3% before fees. Over 100 trades that is about $4,750, yet in 100 trades a 55% loss rate makes a run of about 8 losses in a row likely.

How to use the result

  • Pull your real win rate and average win/loss from your trade log, not from memory. At least 30-50 trades gives a usable estimate.
  • A low win rate is fine if your winners are big: trend followers often win 30-40% of trades with 3R winners.
  • Size each trade so the expected losing streak cannot hurt your account; check that with the risk of ruin calculator and the position size calculator.
  • Once expectancy is positive, the Kelly criterion calculator shows the maximum sensible risk per trade, and the risk reward ratio calculator helps plan each entry.

FAQ

What is a good trading expectancy?

Any positive expectancy after fees means the strategy makes money on average. Many traders aim for at least 0.2R to 0.5R per trade, because slippage and mistakes eat into a thin edge.

How do you calculate expectancy in trading?

Multiply your win rate by your average win, subtract your loss rate times your average loss, then subtract fees. With a 45% win rate, $300 average win and $150 average loss, expectancy is $52.50 before fees.

What is profit factor?

Profit factor is total money won divided by total money lost. A profit factor above 1 is profitable; 1.5 or more is considered solid for most strategies.

How many losing trades in a row should I expect?

Roughly log(number of trades) ÷ log(1 ÷ loss rate). With a 55% loss rate over 100 trades, a streak of about 8 losses is likely at some point, even for a winning system.
