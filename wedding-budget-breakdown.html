Start with the total you can afford without debt, then split it by category. The percentages below are a common starting point; change any of them to match your priorities.

Free wedding budget calculator

How to set your total

  • Add up what you have saved, what you can save each month until the date, and any family contributions that are confirmed (not assumed).
  • Keep 5–10% as a buffer. Almost every wedding has costs nobody budgeted for.
  • Remember the extras: alterations, vendor meals, tips, postage, marriage license and delivery fees.

The guest list is the biggest lever

Catering, rentals, invitations, favors and venue size all scale with headcount. Cutting 20 guests often saves more than any other single change. Check your cost per guest in the calculator above.

Ways to cut costs without it looking cheap

  • Choose a Friday, Sunday or off-season date. Many venues discount these.
  • Pick a venue that includes tables, chairs and linens.
  • Use in-season flowers and reuse ceremony arrangements at the reception.
  • Hire a photographer for fewer hours, covering the ceremony through first dance.
  • Do digital save-the-dates and printed invitations only.

Track deposits and due dates

Most vendors take a deposit of 25–50% at booking and the balance 2–4 weeks before the date. Write every deposit, balance and due date in one place so nothing catches you by surprise in the last month.

FAQ

What percentage of a wedding budget goes to the venue?

Venue and catering together are usually the largest share, commonly around 40-50% of the total budget.

How much does a wedding cost per guest?

Divide your total budget by your guest count. The calculator shows this automatically.
