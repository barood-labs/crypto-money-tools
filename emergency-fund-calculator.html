Emergency Fund Calculator

An emergency fund is cash set aside for job loss, car repairs, medical bills and other surprises, so they don't end up on a credit card. Use the calculator, then read how to size and build yours.

How many months do you need?

SituationSuggested cover
Paying off high-interest debtStarter fund of $1,000 or one month, then focus on debt
Stable job, two incomes, no dependants3 months
Single income, kids, or a home owner6 months
Freelancer, commission, seasonal or contract work6–12 months

What counts as "essential"?

Only what you'd still pay if you lost your income: housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, transport, minimum debt payments, phone and childcare. Leave out eating out, subscriptions and savings contributions. That gives you a smaller, more realistic target.

Where to keep it

Somewhere separate from your checking account, easy to reach in a day or two, and not exposed to market swings. A high-yield savings account is the usual choice. Avoid keeping your emergency fund in stocks or crypto, since prices often fall at the same time people lose jobs.

How to build it faster

  • Automate a transfer on payday, even if it's small.
  • Use a 52-week savings challenge or 100 envelope challenge to make it a game.
  • Send tax refunds, side income and sold items straight to the fund.
  • Refill it after you use it before going back to extra debt payments.

FAQ

How much should be in an emergency fund?

Most guidelines suggest 3 to 6 months of essential expenses, and more if your income is irregular.

Should I pay off debt or build an emergency fund first?

Many people build a small starter fund first, pay off high-interest debt, then grow the fund to 3-6 months.
