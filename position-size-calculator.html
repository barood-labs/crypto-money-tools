Position Size Calculator (Stocks, Crypto and Futures)

How big should your position be? Enter your account size, the percent you are willing to lose on one trade, and your entry and stop. The calculator gives you the number of shares, coins or futures contracts that keeps the loss at your limit if the stop is hit. It runs in your browser.

The formula

Position size = (account balance x risk %) / (distance from entry to stop x value per point). For stocks and crypto the value per point is 1 per unit. For futures it is the contract's dollar value per point, so a 10-point stop on NQ ($20/pt) risks $200 per contract.

Rules of thumb traders use

  • Risk 0.5% to 2% per trade. At 1%, ten losses in a row still leave about 90% of the account.
  • Place the stop where the idea is wrong first, then size the position to it. Never move the stop to fit a bigger size.
  • Round down, never up, when the result is a fraction of a contract.
  • On a prop firm account, size against the drawdown limit, not the account balance. See the prop firm drawdown calculator.

FAQ

How do I calculate position size?

Multiply your account balance by the percent you will risk, then divide by the distance between your entry and stop times the value of one point. Round down to whole contracts or shares.

How much should I risk per trade?

Many traders risk 0.5% to 2% of the account per trade. Smaller risk means a losing streak does less damage.

How many micro contracts equal one E-mini?

Ten. A micro E-mini (MES or MNQ) is one tenth the size of the E-mini (ES or NQ), so it is useful when one full contract is too much risk.
