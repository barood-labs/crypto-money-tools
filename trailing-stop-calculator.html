How a trailing stop works

A trailing stop follows the price in your favour and never moves back. On a long, the stop sits a set distance below the highest price since you entered: stop = highest price × (1 − trail %), or highest price − trail $. On a short, it sits above the lowest price: stop = lowest price × (1 + trail %). When price turns and touches the stop, the position closes. The stop only locks in a profit once price has run far enough: for a long with a percent trail that is break-even exit ÷ (1 − trail %), where the break-even exit after fees is entry × (1 + fee) ÷ (1 − fee).

Worked example

You buy $5,000 of Bitcoin at $60,000 with a 10% trailing stop and 0.1% fees per side. Price runs to $75,000, so the stop is now $67,500. If price falls back and hits it, you make $625 before fees and about $614.38 after fees (12.5% on the move). At the current price of $70,000 the stop is 3.57% away. The stop only starts locking in a profit after Bitcoin trades above about $66,800; before that, a 10% pullback would close the trade at a loss.

Tips for choosing a trail

  • Base the trail on how much the market normally moves, not a round number. The ATR stop loss calculator turns average true range into a stop distance.
  • A wider trail means more risk per coin, so size the trade with the position size calculator.
  • Compare the reward you are trailing for with your initial risk in the risk to reward ratio calculator.
  • On leveraged futures, make sure the stop sits well before your liquidation price.

FAQ

How do you calculate a trailing stop?

For a long, take the highest price since entry and subtract the trail: highest × (1 − trail %) or highest − trail $. For a short, add the trail to the lowest price since entry. Example: a 10% trail with a high of $75,000 puts the stop at $67,500.

What is a good trailing stop percentage for crypto?

There is no single best number. Bitcoin often moves several percent in a day, so very tight trails (1-3%) tend to get hit by noise, while 30%+ gives back a lot. Many traders base the trail on volatility, such as 2-3 times the ATR, and test it on past charts first.

When does a trailing stop lock in profit?

Only after price has moved far enough that the stop is above your break-even exit (below it for a short). With a 10% trail on a $60,000 entry and 0.1% fees, the high has to pass about $66,800 before the stop is at break-even.

Is a trailing stop guaranteed to fill at the stop price?

No. Most trailing stops become market orders when triggered, so in a fast drop or a price gap the fill can be worse than the stop. This calculator assumes a fill exactly at the stop price.

Percent or fixed dollar trail: which is better?

A percent trail scales with price, so it stays the same size relative to the move as the coin rises. A fixed dollar trail gets relatively tighter as price climbs. Percent is more common for crypto; fixed amounts suit instruments that trade in a narrow range.
