How Fibonacci retracement levels are calculated

Take the size of the move, range = swing high − swing low. In an uptrend, traders watch for a pullback, so each retracement level is high − range × ratio. In a downtrend, they watch for a bounce, so each level is low + range × ratio. The ratios come from the Fibonacci sequence: 23.6%, 38.2%, 61.8% and 78.6%, with 50% added by convention even though it is not a Fibonacci ratio. Extensions project past the old high (or low) for profit targets: in an uptrend a 161.8% extension is low + range × 1.618.

Worked example

BTC rallies from $58,000 to $73,000, a $15,000 move. The retracement levels are $69,460 (23.6%), $67,270 (38.2%), $65,500 (50%), $63,730 (61.8%) and $61,210 (78.6%). If the price is now $65,000, it sits just under the 50% level and is heading toward 61.8%, the level many traders call the "golden pocket". The extension targets above the high are $77,080 (127.2%), $82,270 (161.8%) and $97,270 (261.8%).

Using the levels without fooling yourself

  • Fibonacci levels are zones, not exact prices. Look for other reasons a level matters, like old support, a moving average or volume.
  • Different swing points give different levels, so pick clear, obvious highs and lows that other traders can see too.
  • Put your stop beyond the next level, not right on it, and check the trade is worth taking with the risk reward ratio calculator.

FAQ

What are the Fibonacci retracement levels?

The common retracement levels are 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 78.6% of the move from swing low to swing high. 50% is not a true Fibonacci ratio but is included by convention.

How do you calculate a Fibonacci retracement?

Subtract the swing low from the swing high to get the range. In an uptrend, each level is the high minus the range times the ratio; in a downtrend, it is the low plus the range times the ratio.

What is the golden pocket in trading?

The golden pocket is the zone between the 61.8% and 65% retracement levels. Many crypto traders watch it as a likely area for a pullback to find support, but it often fails, so always use a stop.

What are Fibonacci extension levels used for?

Extensions such as 127.2%, 161.8% and 261.8% project beyond the original move and are often used to set profit targets after price breaks the previous high or low.
