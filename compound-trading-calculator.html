How compound trading works

Each period the balance is multiplied by (1 + gain %), so gains are earned on earlier gains too. With no deposits the ending balance is start × (1 + r)n. The number of periods to reach a target is ln(target ÷ start) ÷ ln(1 + r). Monthly deposits are added at the end of each month and then compound with the rest of the account. A losing period is just a negative gain, and because losses compound too, a −10% day needs about +11.1% to get back to even.

Worked example

Start with $1,000 and make an average of 1% a day, every day, for a year (365 days). The balance grows to about $37,783, a profit of $36,783. After the first month (30 days) it is only $1,347.85; the big gains come late, which is why compounding needs time and very consistent returns. Reaching $10,000 takes about 232 days. Drop the average to 0.5% a day and the same year ends at about $6,175. Small changes in the daily rate make huge differences, so use a realistic figure from your own trading journal.

FAQ

How do you calculate compound trading growth?

Multiply the balance by (1 + your average gain per period) once for every period. $1,000 at 1% a day for 365 days is 1,000 × 1.01^365, about $37,783.

Is 1% a day realistic?

Very few traders keep it up. 1% a day compounds to more than 3,600% a year, and real results include losing days and drawdowns. Use your own journal's average to get a realistic projection.

How long does it take to double a trading account?

Divide ln(2) by ln(1 + r). At 1% per period it takes about 70 periods; at 0.5% about 139; at 2% about 35. The rule of 72 gives a quick estimate: 72 ÷ the % gain per period.

Do losses compound too?

Yes. A 10% loss needs an 11.1% gain to get back to even, and a 50% loss needs 100%. That is why limiting risk per trade matters more than chasing a high daily return.

Should I withdraw profits or compound them?

Compounding grows the account faster, but withdrawing part of the profit locks in gains. Enter a negative monthly deposit to see how regular withdrawals change the ending balance.
