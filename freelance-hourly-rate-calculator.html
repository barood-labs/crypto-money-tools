Freelance Hourly Rate Calculator

The most common freelance mistake is taking your old salary, dividing by 2,080 hours and charging that. Freelancers pay their own taxes, benefits and software, take unpaid time off, and spend a big chunk of each week on work nobody pays for (sales, admin, invoicing). This calculator accounts for all of it.

What goes into "business costs"

Software and subscriptions, equipment, a computer replacement fund, phone and internet share, coworking, accounting help, insurance, training, marketing and payment processing fees (often 3% on card payments).

Why only 60–70% of hours are billable

Finding clients, writing proposals, email, invoicing and bookkeeping are real work, but nobody pays an hourly rate for them. New freelancers often bill only 50% of their working hours.

Should you charge hourly or per project?

Use your hourly number as a floor, then price projects by estimating hours × rate × 1.2 for scope creep. As you get faster, project pricing lets you keep the upside instead of being penalized for efficiency.

FAQ

How do I calculate my freelance hourly rate?

Add your target income, business costs, benefits and taxes, then divide by the hours you can actually bill in a year after time off and unbilled work.

How much should a freelancer set aside for taxes?

Many US freelancers set aside 25-35% of profit, but it depends on income and location. See our self-employment tax set-aside calculator.
