How crypto arbitrage profit is calculated

Buy on the cheaper exchange: coins = trade size × (1 − buy fee) ÷ buy price. Withdraw them: coins arriving = coins − withdrawal fee. Sell on the dearer exchange: proceeds = coins arriving × sell price × (1 − sell fee). Net profit = proceeds − trade size. The withdrawal fee is a fixed number of coins, so it costs the same on a $1,000 trade as on a $100,000 one. That is why small arbitrage trades often lose money even when the % fees look tiny, and why the calculator shows the smallest trade size that breaks even.

Worked example

Bitcoin is $60,000 on exchange A and $60,300 on exchange B, a 0.5% gap. You trade $10,000 with 0.1% taker fees on both sides and a 0.0002 BTC withdrawal fee. You buy 0.1665 BTC, 0.1663 BTC arrives, and selling it brings in $10,017.86: a net profit of $17.86 (0.18%) after $32.09 of fees. The gap has to be at least 0.32% (a $60,192.48 sell price) to break even, and any trade under about $4,028 loses money. The same trade at $1,000 loses $9.06. If Bitcoin slips just 0.3% on exchange B while the coins are moving, the $10,000 trade turns into a $12.19 loss.

FAQ

How do you calculate crypto arbitrage profit?

Buy coins on the cheaper exchange (trade size minus the buy fee, divided by the price), subtract the withdrawal fee in coins, then sell the rest on the dearer exchange and take off the sell fee. Net profit is what you get back minus what you put in. Example: $10,000 across a 0.5% Bitcoin gap with 0.1% fees each side and a 0.0002 BTC withdrawal fee nets about $17.86.

Is crypto arbitrage still profitable?

Sometimes, but the gaps between big exchanges are usually small and short-lived because bots close them fast. After taker fees on both sides, a withdrawal fee and the time coins spend in transit, many visible gaps are a loss. Run the numbers on each one first.

What spread do I need for arbitrage to be worth it?

At least the total of your buy fee, sell fee and the withdrawal fee as a share of the trade. With 0.1% fees on each side and a 0.0002 BTC withdrawal fee, a $10,000 Bitcoin trade needs a gap of about 0.32% just to break even; a $1,000 trade needs about 1.42%.

What is triangular arbitrage?

Triangular (market) arbitrage trades three pairs on one exchange, for example USDT to BTC, BTC to ETH and ETH back to USDT, to profit from a mismatch between their prices. It skips the withdrawal fee but pays a trading fee three times. This calculator covers the two-exchange kind.

Is crypto arbitrage risk-free?

No. Prices can move while coins are in transit, an exchange can pause withdrawals, large orders fill at worse prices than the quote, and some exchanges limit or freeze accounts. Use the price-drop field to see how quickly a small move wipes out the profit.
