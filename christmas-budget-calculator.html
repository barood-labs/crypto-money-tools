How to set a Christmas budget in 4 steps

  1. Start with what you can afford, not with the list. Look at what is left after bills over the next two months. If the calculator total is bigger than that number, trim the list now, not at the register.
  2. Set a per-person limit. A fixed number per group (kids, family, friends, coworkers) makes deciding fast and keeps things fair.
  3. Budget the hidden costs. Food, wrapping, postage, travel and party outfits often add 40-60% on top of gifts. They're in the calculator so they don't surprise you.
  4. Track as you buy. Write down the actual price of every gift. Tracking is what keeps a budget real.

Ways to spend less without looking cheap

  • Suggest a family Secret Santa or white elephant swap: one good gift each instead of ten small ones.
  • Make a Black Friday list before the sales and only buy what's on it. Our Black Friday discount calculator checks whether a deal is real.
  • Give experiences or homemade food. Cookie boxes and a handwritten note are often remembered longest.
  • Use a Christmas sinking fund next year so December is already paid for.

FAQ

How much should I spend on Christmas?

There is no single right number. A common rule of thumb is to keep total holiday spending at or below about 1.5% of your yearly take-home pay, and to only spend money you have already saved. Gifts are usually around half of the total.

How much should I spend per person on Christmas gifts?

Many families use tiers, for example more for a partner and kids, a set amount for parents and siblings, and $10-25 for friends, coworkers and teachers. Pick the numbers that fit your total budget.

How much do I need to save each week for Christmas?

Take your total Christmas budget, subtract what you have already saved, and divide by the number of weeks left until December 25. The calculator above does this for you.
