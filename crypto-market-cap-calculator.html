How the market cap price is calculated

Market cap is price × circulating supply. Turn it around and the price a coin would trade at for a given market cap is target market cap ÷ circulating supply. The multiple from today is target cap ÷ current cap. Fully diluted value (FDV) uses the max or total supply instead: price × max supply. A big gap between market cap and FDV means many tokens are still to be unlocked or emitted, which can dilute holders.

Worked example

A token trades at $0.50 with 1 billion tokens circulating, so its market cap is $500 million. At a $5 billion market cap with the same supply it would be $5.00, a 10×. With 2 billion max supply its FDV today is already $1 billion, and if all 2 billion were circulating, a $5 billion cap would mean only $2.50 per token. Holding 2,000 tokens, that is $1,000 today, $10,000 at a $5B cap on today's supply, or $5,000 fully diluted.

Reality checks before you dream

  • Market cap is not money that flowed in. Thin order books can move cap a lot on small volume, and it can fall just as fast.
  • Supply changes. Check unlock schedules, emissions and burns; use the FDV line to see the fully diluted price.
  • Compare against real coins of the same type and age, not just Bitcoin or Ethereum.

FAQ

How do you calculate the price of a crypto at a certain market cap?

Divide the target market cap by the circulating supply. For example, a token with 1 billion tokens circulating would trade at $5.00 at a $5 billion market cap, assuming supply stays the same.

What is the difference between market cap and fully diluted value?

Market cap uses the circulating supply, while fully diluted value (FDV) uses the maximum or total supply. If FDV is much higher than market cap, a lot of tokens are still to be released, which can push the price down as they enter circulation.

Can a coin realistically reach a big market cap?

This calculator only does the math; it is not a prediction. Reaching a larger market cap needs real demand and liquidity, and supply unlocks can work against price. Compare with coins of a similar type and stage before assuming a multiple.
