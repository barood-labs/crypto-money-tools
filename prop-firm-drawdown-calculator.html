Trailing vs static drawdown

A static drawdown stays at a fixed balance (for example $48,000 on a $50,000 account). A trailing drawdown follows your highest balance up, so every new high moves the floor up too, and many firms stop trailing once the floor reaches the starting balance. Trailing limits are why big winning days followed by a giveback breach so many accounts. Always read your own firm's exact rule: end-of-day and intraday trailing behave differently.

FAQ

What is a trailing drawdown in a prop firm?

It is a loss limit that moves up as your account balance makes new highs. If your balance falls back by the drawdown amount from its peak, the account fails. Many firms stop trailing once the limit reaches the starting balance.

How much should I risk per trade on a prop firm account?

A common approach is to keep each trade's risk small enough that 8 to 10 losses in a row would not hit the drawdown, which is often 10% to 12% of the drawdown per trade or less.

Can I pass a prop firm challenge faster by risking more?

Bigger risk reaches the target in fewer winning trades but makes a breach much more likely during a normal losing streak. The calculator shows both numbers so you can choose.
