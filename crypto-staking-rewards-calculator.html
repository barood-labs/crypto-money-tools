How staking rewards are calculated

First take the fee out of the yield: net rate = APY x (1 - fee). A 7% APY with a 10% validator commission is really 6.3%. If rewards are restaked, end coins = staked x (1 + net rate / n)n x years, where n is how many times a year rewards compound. If they are not restaked, rewards grow in a straight line: staked x net rate x years. The dollar value then depends on the coin's price when you sell, which matters far more than the APY.

Worked example

Stake 10 SOL at $150 with a 7% APY and a 10% fee for 12 months, compounding daily. The net rate is 6.3%, so you end with about 10.65 SOL, roughly 0.65 SOL of rewards worth about $98 at today's price. If SOL falls 20% over the year, your stake is worth less than you started with even after rewards. Staking yield does not protect you from price drops.

Taxes on staking rewards (US)

  • The IRS treats staking rewards as ordinary income at their fair market value when you gain control of them (Revenue Ruling 2023-14).
  • That value becomes your cost basis for those coins, so when you later sell, swap or spend them you also have a capital gain or loss.
  • Lots of small daily rewards make record keeping painful, which is why most stakers use a tracker or crypto tax software. Work out gains on a later sale with the crypto profit calculator and cost basis with the cost basis guide.

Staking risks to weigh

  • Price risk: a 5-10% yield is wiped out by a small price drop.
  • Lock-ups and unbonding: some chains take days or weeks to unstake.
  • Platform risk: exchange staking means the exchange holds your coins; see our crypto wallet guide.
  • Slashing: a misbehaving validator can lose part of the stake on some networks.

FAQ

How are crypto staking rewards calculated?

Multiply your stake by the APY after the validator or platform fee. If rewards are restaked they compound, so the effective yield is a little higher than the rate; if not, rewards grow in a straight line.

What is the difference between APR and APY in staking?

APR is the simple yearly rate without compounding. APY includes the effect of restaking rewards, so it is higher than the APR whenever rewards compound.

Are staking rewards taxable?

In the US, yes. The IRS treats them as ordinary income at their market value when you receive them, and selling them later can also create a capital gain or loss.
