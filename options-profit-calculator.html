Options Profit Calculator

Work out what an options trade can make or lose before you place it. Pick a call or put, buy or sell, and enter the stock or crypto price, strike, days to expiry and implied volatility. The calculator prices the option with the Black-Scholes model, shows the break-even price, max profit and max loss, the Greeks (delta, gamma, theta and vega), the chance of finishing in the money, and a profit table at expiration. Works for stock options (100 shares per contract) and crypto options such as BTC and ETH (set the multiplier to 1). Runs in your browser.




How the options profit calculator works

At expiration a call is worth max(0, price − strike) and a put is worth max(0, strike − price). Your profit is that value minus the premium you paid, times the multiplier and number of contracts (reversed if you sold). The break-even price is strike + premium for a call and strike − premium for a put. Before expiry, the Black-Scholes model estimates a fair premium from the price, strike, time, implied volatility and interest rate, and gives the Greeks: delta (price change per $1 move), gamma (how fast delta changes), theta (time decay per day) and vega (price change per 1 point of volatility).

Worked example

A stock trades at $100. You buy one $105 call expiring in 30 days with implied volatility of 40% and a 4% rate. Black-Scholes prices it at about $2.71, so one contract costs $271. Your break-even at expiry is $107.71, and the model gives roughly a 32% chance of the stock finishing above $105. Delta is 0.37 (about $37 per contract for each $1 move), and theta costs about $7.57 a day. If the stock is at $115 at expiry you make (10 − 2.71) × 100 = $729; anywhere below $105 you lose the full $271.

FAQ

How do you calculate profit on an option?

At expiration, profit = (intrinsic value − premium paid) × 100 × contracts for a bought stock option. A call's intrinsic value is price minus strike, a put's is strike minus price, and neither can go below zero.

What is the break-even price of an option?

For a call, strike plus the premium paid. For a put, strike minus the premium paid. A $105 call bought for $2.71 breaks even at $107.71 at expiration.

What is the Black-Scholes model?

A formula that prices European-style options from the underlying price, strike, time to expiry, implied volatility and interest rate. It also produces the Greeks, which show how the option's price reacts to each input.

Can I use this for Bitcoin or Ethereum options?

Yes. Enter the coin price, strike, days to expiry and implied volatility, and set the multiplier to 1 (one coin per contract). Crypto implied volatility is often 40-80%, much higher than most stocks.

What does theta mean?

Theta is how much value the option loses each day from time decay, all else equal. It speeds up as expiration gets closer, which hurts option buyers and helps sellers.
