How Much Should I Set Aside for Taxes If I'm Self-Employed?

When you're self-employed in the US, no employer withholds tax for you. You owe self-employment tax (Social Security and Medicare) on top of income tax, and the IRS generally expects estimated payments four times a year. This calculator gives a rough set-aside amount. It's an estimate for planning, not tax advice.

Free set-aside estimator


Self-employment tax is estimated as 15.3% of 92.35% of net profit (Social Security portion is capped at high incomes; not modeled here). Income tax is applied to profit minus half of SE tax as a simplification. Ignores deductions, credits and other income.

Quarterly estimated tax due dates

Income earnedUsual due date
Jan 1 – Mar 31April 15
Apr 1 – May 31June 15
Jun 1 – Aug 31September 15
Sep 1 – Dec 31January 15 (next year)

If a date falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day. Check the IRS page for Form 1040-ES each year.

A simple system

  1. Open a separate savings account just for taxes.
  2. Every time a client pays you, move your set-aside percentage there right away.
  3. Pay the quarterly estimate from that account.
  4. Track business expenses all year; they lower your profit and your tax.

Track what you can deduct

Common deductions include software, equipment, a portion of phone and internet, home office (if you qualify), professional services and business mileage. Keep receipts and a running log rather than reconstructing everything in April.

FAQ

How much should I set aside for taxes as a freelancer?

Many US freelancers save 25-35% of profit. Use the calculator for a rough estimate and confirm with a tax professional.

What is the self-employment tax rate?

15.3% (12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare), applied to 92.35% of net self-employment earnings, with the Social Security part capped at higher incomes.
