How crypto tax-loss harvesting works

  1. Losses offset gains of the same type first. Short-term losses reduce short-term gains (taxed like wages); long-term losses reduce long-term gains.
  2. Then they cross over. Leftover losses of one type offset gains of the other.
  3. Up to $3,000 of net loss ($1,500 if married filing separately) can reduce ordinary income each year.
  4. The rest carries forward to future years with no expiry.

Does the wash-sale rule apply to crypto?

As of the 2026 tax year, the wash-sale rule (IRC section 1091) covers stocks and securities. Most crypto is treated as property, so selling at a loss and buying back soon after does not automatically disallow the loss. Congress has proposed extending the rule to digital assets, and tokenized stocks are already covered, so check the current rule before you sell and rebuy, and keep records of both trades.

Year-end checklist

  • Pull your realized gains so far from each exchange (many have a tax or gains report).
  • List positions where the value is below your cost basis, per wallet or account.
  • Sell by December 31 in your time zone; the trade date counts, not the settlement date.
  • Save the trade confirmations. Your 1099-DA should show the proceeds early next year.

FAQ

Can I sell crypto at a loss and buy it back right away?

For the 2026 tax year, the wash-sale rule covers stocks and securities, and most crypto is treated as property, so an immediate rebuy generally does not disallow the loss. Proposals to change this exist, so confirm the rule for your tax year with a tax professional.

How much crypto loss can I deduct?

Losses first offset your capital gains with no limit. If losses exceed gains, up to $3,000 per year ($1,500 if married filing separately) can offset other income, and the rest carries forward to future years.

When is the deadline for crypto tax-loss harvesting?

The sale must happen by December 31 to count for that tax year.
