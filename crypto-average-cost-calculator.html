How the average cost is calculated

Your average cost per coin is the total dollars spent divided by the total coins you own. It is a weighted average, so a big buy at one price moves it more than a small buy at another. Buying 0.05 BTC at $60,000 and 0.2 BTC at an average of $37,500 gives 0.25 BTC for $10,500, an average of $42,000, not the simple $45,000 average of the prices.

How to average down

To bring your average down to a target, you need to buy more coins below that target. The coins needed are (current average - target) x coins held / (target - current price). The closer the target is to today's price, the more you have to buy, and a target at or below today's price can never be reached. Averaging down lowers your break-even price, but it also adds to a position that is losing money, so set a limit before you start.

Average cost and taxes

In the US, the IRS doesn't use a single average cost for crypto. Each sale uses the cost basis of the specific coins sold (FIFO by default unless you identify lots). FAQ

How do I calculate my average crypto buy price?

Add up every dollar you spent on the coin, including fees if you want them counted, then divide by the total number of coins you bought. That gives your weighted average cost per coin.

How much do I need to buy to average down?

Coins needed = (current average - target average) x coins held / (target average - current price). The target has to be above today's price, and the closer it is, the more you need to buy.

Is average cost the same as cost basis for taxes?

Not in the US. Crypto sales use the cost basis of the specific lots sold, FIFO by default. Average cost is useful for tracking break-even, but your tax report needs lot-level records.
