How the Kelly Criterion works

The classic Kelly fraction is f* = W − (1 − W) / R, where W is your win rate as a decimal and R is avg win / avg loss. A positive f* means you have an edge; a negative f* means the setup loses money on average and Kelly says bet nothing. Full Kelly maximizes long-run growth but is very volatile, so most traders use half or quarter Kelly to cut drawdowns.

Kelly vs fixed percent risk

Kelly tells you the theoretically optimal risk for a known edge. In live trading your win rate and payoff are estimates, so treat the number as a ceiling and round down. FAQ

What is the Kelly Criterion formula for trading?

f* = W − (1 − W) / R, where W is win rate (0–1) and R is average win divided by average loss. Multiply by 0.5 for half Kelly or 0.25 for quarter Kelly.

Should I use full Kelly or half Kelly?

Full Kelly is aggressive and produces large drawdowns. Most traders use half or quarter Kelly so a string of losses does less damage while still growing the account when the edge is real.

What if Kelly says a negative percent?

A negative Kelly means your estimated win rate and payoff have no edge. Do not size up: tighten the stop, widen the target, improve selection, or skip the setup.
